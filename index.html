WHAT IS AN ENZYME?

An enzyme is a protein that acts as a biological catalyst — it speeds up chemical reactions inside living cells without being used up. Every enzyme has a special pocket called the active site that is shaped to fit only specific molecules, called substrates.

🔵 ENZYME 1 — NONPOLAR ACTIVE SITE
Its active site is round and hydrophobic. It only binds nonpolar amino acids — the ones that avoid water and hide inside proteins.
🟣 ENZYME 2 — POLAR ACTIVE SITE
Its active site is angular and charged. It only binds polar and charged amino acids — the ones that interact with water.

🔑 This is called lock-and-key specificity — just like a key only fits one lock, each enzyme only works with substrates that match its active site shape and charge. If the wrong substrate enters the active site, the reaction doesn't happen.
